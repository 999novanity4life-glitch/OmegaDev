## Warehouse ("Northgate Distribution", photoreal)
| Layer | What is there | Why it reads as quality |
|---|---|---|
| Structure rhythm | Steel columns every bay, each with a yellow/black guard sleeve at the base; truss roof; skylight strips; ~20 pendant high-bay lamps in a grid going to the vanishing point | Repetition at a fixed interval sells scale and depth. The eye counts bays. |
| Hero accent | A red shipping container hanging mid-air from a yellow gantry crane | One saturated object against a neutral grey/white room. Overhead, so the frame has a top layer. |
| Overhead layer | Crane beam, sagging cables, lamps, skylights | Most AI maps leave everything above head height empty. Sagging cables break the CG straight lines. |
| Floor as canvas | Yellow lane lines, white dashed lines, a painted bay rectangle, worn concrete | The floor carries information and leading lines, not one flat material. |
| Props in story groups | Pallets of boxes in clumps of 3-8 with different heights; full racks packed along the left wall; a forklift and a pallet jack mid-task; blue drums; a red extinguisher on a pillar | Dense at the edges, open in the middle. Vehicles caught mid-job say "people work here". |
| Trim and edges | Guard sleeves, rack beams, door frames, kick plates, corrugated container walls | Every edge gets a secondary piece. Detail = seams and edges, not more objects. |
| Light | Warm daylight from skylights, cool fill, haze deepening with distance, bloom on lamps, dark contact shadows under pallets | Aerial perspective gives depth; contact shadows glue objects to the floor. |
| Camera | Wide, near eye height, lane lines running to the vanishing point | Composition does half the work. |

## Recording studio ("Mic Drop Studios", control room + corridor)
- **Micro density on one hero object:** the mixing console has dozens of knobs and faders in rows (100+ tiny repeats). The rest of the room is calm. Detail is concentrated, not spread.
- **Material pairs:** warm wood desk and floor vs matte black panels and grey acoustic foam vs glossy screens. At least 4 materials in one shot, each with a clear job.
- **Wall treatment:** wood slat diffusers, foam panels in a grid, monitors as emissive rectangles.
- **Corridor:** wall panels in a grid with visible seams; a blue LED cove line along the ceiling edge running to the vanishing point; wood door frames at a steady rhythm with framed art between doors; one purple carpet room as the accent; downlights in a row; a glossy floor reflecting it all.

## Stylised outdoor maps
- **Mistvale Lake:** a terraced cliff bowl rings the whole play area (natural boundary, no invisible wall); a waterfall on the far wall is the weenie; cabins cluster on ledges, not spread evenly; a sand crescent is the transition band between grass and water; a pier pokes into the lake; one big hero tree frames the foreground.
- **Sugarcrest Kingdom:** huge rounded macro hills with drips (macro), a castle as the landmark, a ribbon path leading to it, small candies scattered near the camera (micro).
- **The Gilded Dunes:** a stepped pyramid landmark, broken ruin walls at meso scale, a lone palm and pots as micro.
- **Bloomcrest Care Centre:** a round building on a round plaza; curved paths; hedges and pink trees line the paths.

## The method underneath (what we copy)
1. Neutral base + one saturated hero accent per shot.
2. Rhythm: structural pieces repeat at a fixed interval and run to a vanishing point.
3. Trim every edge: sleeves, frames, seams, kick plates, cove lights.
4. Overhead layer: something above head height in every shot.
5. Floor is a canvas: markings, wear, material bands.
6. Props in story groups of 3-8 with height variation, packed along edges, open floor in the middle.
7. Micro density piled onto one hero object, calm elsewhere.
8. Curves and sags (cables, ropes) break straight CG lines.
9. Emissive lines lead the eye.
10. Material contrast pairs (warm wood vs cool metal, matte vs gloss).
11. Haze in the distance, bloom on sources, contact shadows under objects.
12. Outdoor: a terraced bowl encloses the space; the weenie sits on the far wall.
