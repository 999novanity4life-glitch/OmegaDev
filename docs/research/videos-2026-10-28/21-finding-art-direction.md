# Finding Your Art Direction | RDC 2024
- Channel: Roblox | URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWx8UTxsi0A | Length: 36:58 | Upload: 2024-09-23 | Captions: auto
Speaker: Gino Roy (Heart_Hertz), art director at Sawhorse Interactive (film, music-video and Disney animation background).

- [4:15] The four pillars are concept art, world building, lighting and color. They are powerful when used deliberately but "often skipped and left to chance."
- [4:46] Visual concept: choose a motif (a repeating pattern) that shapes your style and reinforces the story.
- [6:19] Start with sketches before you have assets. For Kung Fu Panda the team went frame by frame through the trailer, sketched three buildings, and built a whole city from them. Use sketches to call out details to get exactly right.
- [7:21] A sketch can be done anywhere; the goal is communicating the idea to the team.
- [7:51] "Take time to save time": gameplay was approved from an overhead map. Redrawing a map is far cheaper than undoing a built city.
- [8:21] Draw your own reference instead of relying on online images. Found references are often slightly wrong in color or style, which costs revisions.
- [10:24] A project has four stages: concept art, graybox, art build, finished map. The first two are often skipped.
- [10:55] Graybox: lay out the whole world as gray boxes, playtest before the scene is built, move things on the fly, then replace boxes with art.
- [11:56] Scale with landmarks. Identify the main gameplay areas, count them, and check travel time between them so players don't lose interest or get lost.
- [12:27] Build from the player's POV, like setting up a stop-motion camera. Compose the view from the spawn point, make the next destination prominent and close enough, then repeat at each landmark ("where's the next place I need to be?").
- [13:00] Those composed POV shots also give you free thumbnails.
- [14:00] When expanding a brand or IP into a full world, add "a piece of you", drawn from what excites you.
- [15:30] Storytelling with light: treat the game as a journey with a beginning, middle and end, and each landmark as a movie scene with its own mood and lighting.
- [17:01] Light instantly sets time of day and tone, adds drama, guides the way, directs focus. Placed lights off versus on changed the scene drastically, and the lighting alone built in a "get from here to there" goal.
- [18:03] For cinematic lighting: use Atmosphere settings to separate background from foreground, optionally a DepthOfField effect, and SunRays with a sunset behind the scene.
- [19:06] To learn, put a still from a film you love next to Studio and match the light direction, shadow placement and color treatment.
- [20:09] Fake global illumination: add a few extra lights where window light would bounce (one on the floor, one on the wall). Don't "quadruple all your lights". Only at a few important places.
- [21:43] For cluttered, dark interiors, turn off shadows on overhead objects and anything else blocking light.
- [22:14] Tool: the "Celestial Body Dragger" plugin (by Maximum_ADHD) lets you drag the sun or moon instead of tuning ClockTime and GeographicLatitude.
- [23:15] The skybox is huge and tints every color in the scene. Counterbalance colors that must stay exact, and break up a dominant tint with neon accents.
- [25:52] Limit the palette to 2-3 colors for a softer, relaxed mood. A single color reads as very dramatic.
- [26:53] Color for gameplay: players read saturated red, green or blue as signals from other games. Testers avoided areas that looked like lava and ran into green bushes instead of collecting items, so playtest your palette for unintended cues.
- [31:31] Light budget: no fixed number. Add lights, then toggle each one to see if it's needed. Guidance is that light radii shouldn't overlap. He stretches range up to 60, then removes what isn't needed.
- [32:02] Tool: the "Lighting Editor" plugin (by Woot3) lists every light with its type and color, used to find hotspots such as 18 lights stacked in one spot.
- [33:35] Team communication: kickoff meeting; Milanote board for art direction and task mapping.

Applies to Roblox how (inferred): Graybox each zone and check landmark-to-landmark travel times before art. Give each zone its own Lighting/Atmosphere/Sky preset, swapped as the player arrives. Keep saturated red, orange and green out of decor so they stay reserved for hazards and pickups, and audit light counts.
