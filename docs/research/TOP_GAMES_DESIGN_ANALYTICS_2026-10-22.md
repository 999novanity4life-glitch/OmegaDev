# Steal a Dream Must Win Return Visits

"Steal a Dream" sits in the strongest loop on Roblox right now, and in its most crowded one. The steal-collect-defend formula produced the two largest player peaks of 2025–26: Steal a Brainrot's 25.4M concurrent users in October 2025 and Steal An Egg's 14.3M in September 2026. Each skin of that loop has faded within about six months, though, and Roblox changed its recommendation system in June and August 2026 to stop rewarding exactly that spike-and-crash pattern. Discovery now depends on play-through rate, low bounce in the first three minutes, and players coming back on days 2–7 and 8–28. Short-term monetization counts against a game. The lessons from the top games therefore come in three parts. Borrow the loop: passive-income collectibles, a base that can be raided, a shield, rarity tiers and weekly events. Add the offline-growth hook that made Grow a Garden work, which "falling asleep" supplies naturally. Spend design effort on the first three minutes and the reason to return tomorrow, not on lore. On the visual side, the bedroom fantasy works if the creator holds one discipline: a cool, quiet room with warm lamp pools, where only the dream creatures and icons are saturated and bright. That is the same trick the curated renders on @blender.community use. The plan at the end orders the first build work as a Palette module, two Lighting presets, and about a dozen server-side analytics events. It also gives a six-week learning path so the creator can build each piece himself.

## The steal loop keeps winning, but each skin lasts about six months

As of October 2026 the chart leader is **Steal An Egg**, launched July 25, 2026. It took **#1 in Q3 2026 with 6B visits and a 14.2M peak CCU**. Murder Mystery 2 and +1 Speed Keyboard Escape followed at about 4B visits each, and Steal a Brainrot fell to #5 after leading Q2 ([OtakuKart summarizing Aream & Co.](https://otakukart.com/robloxs-steal-an-egg-reaches-6-billion-visits-and-14-2-million-peak-concurrent-players/)). RoStats records its all-time peak at **14,313,917 on 2026-09-19** ([RoStats](https://rostats.io/records)), but another tracker says 12.4M ([rblxstat](https://rblxstat.com/lists/record-breaking-roblox-games)), and RoWatcher and Rotrends list only 2.2M and 1.2M, so treat every peak figure as approximate. A live pull of Roblox's own charts on October 13 had Steal An Egg at about 928K players, roughly three times the #2 game, and #1 on Top Earning ahead of RIVALS, Adopt Me!, Blox Fruits and Dandy's World ([Roblox explore API](https://apis.roblox.com/explore-api/v1/get-sorts)). Under the spikes sit the evergreens. **Brookhaven and Blox Fruits were in the top 10 every month of 2025**, and Brookhaven leads lifetime visits at roughly 82–87B (tracker figures, undated; the Newzoo write-up gives no lifetime total) ([Newzoo via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/newzoo-top-roblox-games-in-2025)). Roblox does not publish per-game earnings, so any earnings ranking beyond its own unvalued Top Earning chart is an estimate.

The hits are built the same way, and the design is simple. In **Steal a Brainrot**, characters ride a conveyor across the map. Players buy them and store them in their base, where they produce passive income. Progress comes from buying rarer ones or **stealing from other bases**, and a base-lock button gives a temporary shield ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). **Steal An Egg** reached 14M by re-theming that loop around eggs and pets. It added hatching, size and mutation rolls, and a speed stat to train ([OtakuKart](https://otakukart.com/robloxs-steal-an-egg-reaches-6-billion-visits-and-14-2-million-peak-concurrent-players/)). **Grow a Garden** contributed the other key hook: **crops keep growing while you are offline**, and the stock rotates, with weekly exclusives that can only be claimed online ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). Its creator gave two reasons for its success: the theme is universal ("Everyone likes to farm, to watch something grow"), and offline growth was new on Roblox. The game went from 500 to about 100K CCU in two weeks once weekly updates began ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/janzen-madsen-interview/)). **99 Nights in the Forest** began as a week-long project and was built in a three-month sprint by a core team of five. It kept part, vertex and instance counts low for mobile and tuned its deer mascot to be "scary without ostracizing younger users." It ships weekly, with a 45-minute weekend sandbox "Update Party" before each update ([DevForum Creator Spotlight](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creator-spotlight-the-story-behind-99-nights-in-the-forest/4036940)).

**Storyline barely matters to the biggest spikes.** Steal a Brainrot has no plot. Its drama comes from events such as the admin war with Grow a Garden, which set the **platform record of 47.4M concurrent users on 2025-08-23**, and a Bruno Mars concert that drew 12.8M ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). Every named Grow a Garden update (Blood Moon, Bizzy Bees, Summer) set a new CCU record ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). 99 Nights limits its lore to cultists and a mascot, yet that was enough for a 20th Century Studios film deal ([Pryor Cashman](https://www.pryorcashman.com/news/pryor-cashman-client-grandmas-favourite-games-99-nights-in-the-forest-headed-to-the-big-screen)). A memorable creature roster and a calendar of live events do the job that story does elsewhere. Steal a Dream does not need a written plot. It needs dream creatures worth showing off and a recurring "Nightmare Night" event.

The genre's weakness is retention. One practitioner report, method unverified, puts brainrot-category **D1 retention at 18% against a 32% platform average, and D7 at 4% against 14%** ([RoLearn](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/q2-2025-market-trends)). Steal a Brainrot fell 99.2% from its peak by about late March 2026 (date inferred from the article's references), and "games that stop updating die within weeks" ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/is-the-brainrot-trend-dying-what-ccu-data-from-three-top-games-reveals)). Grow a Garden was out of the top 10 by December 2025, about four months after its August peak ([Newzoo](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/newzoo-top-roblox-games-in-2025)). The known complaints are pay-to-win, with Polygon noting "the best items are only available for cash," plus exploit scripts and viral clips of children crying after a theft ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). For Steal a Dream, the lesson is that being a copy is not what kills these games. Clones fail because they bring no new collectible fantasy, no new mechanic and no weekly updates. Falling asleep works as both the new mechanic and an offline-progress timer: dreams can incubate while the player is away. Theft also needs protection for losers and new players from day one, such as shields, partial loss or insurance, because younger players quit over losing a rare creature.

One constraint shapes all of this. New games now launch **only to age-checked players 16+**. They reach the under-16 audience after the creator is age-verified (ID or facial estimation), has 2FA, an account in good standing, a Maturity & Compliance rating of Moderate or lower, and either a one-time refundable publishing fee or two months of Roblox Plus/Premium, and the game has **250 unique plays from "highly engaged" age-checked players within 60 days** ([Roblox Docs: Kids and Select](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/kids-and-select)). A cozy kids' fantasy therefore has to hold teenage and adult testers first. Any paid random item, such as an egg, a dream roll or a luck boost, must show its odds before purchase ([Roblox Docs: Paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)).

## Roblox's 2026 algorithm pays for day-28 returns, not clicks

On **June 15, 2026**, Roblox widened "Recommended For You" signals from a 7-day to a **28-day window** (D1, D2–7, D8–28). It also **removed qualified play-through rate** and replaced it with plain **play-through rate (PTR)** and **first play bounce rate**. Roblox said short-term engagement had let "thumbnail-driven" games crowd out games with lasting value ([DevForum announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/recommended-for-you-algorithm-improvements-that-better-value-long-term-retention/4684575)). An August 6, 2026 follow-up added that "Monetization alone can't create a sustainable game" and told creators with weak retention to fix core gameplay before monetizing ([DevForum: Boost Your Discovery](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/boost-your-discovery-by-building-games-people-want-to-play/4779042)). The official signal list is below, with the creator's lever for each ([Roblox Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery)).

| Priority | Signal | What it means for Steal a Dream |
|---|---|---|
| Most important | Play-through rate | Thumbnail and title must sell "steal glowing dream creatures from a bedroom" honestly |
| Most important | First play bounce rate (sessions under 60 s, and 61–180 s) | The first fall-asleep and first creature catch must happen within about 2 minutes |
| Most important | Play days per user (D1, D2–7, D8–28) | Offline dream incubation, rotating creature stock, daily rewards |
| Most important | Playtime per user, **capped at 60 min/day** | Time past an hour earns no algorithm credit |
| Important | Intentional co-play days | Friend invites, team raids, private servers |
| Important | Qualified sessions, spend days, Robux spent | Small, fair purchases over many days beat one big pay-to-win spend |

Only players who arrive organically from Recommended For You count. Signals are **per-user averages**, so a small game with an engaged cohort is not disadvantaged. A **custom thumbnail is required** to get Home impressions at all, and games that closely resemble existing games are shown less ([Roblox Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery)). That last rule is another reason to make dream creatures and bedrooms look clearly different from eggs and brainrots. Payouts changed too. Engagement-Based Payouts (the old Premium Payouts) was replaced by **Creator Rewards, announced July 24, 2025**. Its Daily Engagement Reward pays 5 Robux when an "Active Spender" ($9.99+ in 60 days) plays your game for 10+ minutes in a day, counting only the first three games that player launches that day ([DevForum: Creator Rewards](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-creator-rewards-earn-more-by-growing-the-community/3777628)). Both the algorithm and the payouts favour daily return visits over marathon sessions.

Creator Hub already shows **D1/D7 retention, average session time, PTR, payer conversion, ARPPU and ARPDAU**, plus an Insights panel that flags large moves. Full KPIs unlock after **more than 10 DAU and 10 play hours for 7 consecutive days** ([Roblox Docs: Analytics dashboard](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/analytics-dashboard)). Roblox publishes no fixed retention benchmarks. Its benchmarks compare against similar games that have at least 100 DAU; the docs give no DAU threshold your own game must reach to see them. Until then, practitioner ranges such as D1 25–40% and D7 10–20% are rough, genre-dependent targets ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/retention-benchmarks-by-roblox-genre-what-good-actually-looks-like)).

Finding the moments where players light up or leave takes custom instrumentation through `AnalyticsService`. Use the **`Log*` methods**; the `Fire*` methods are deprecated. Events fire **only from the server, in a published game**, and dashboards lag up to 24 hours ([API reference](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/AnalyticsService)). An onboarding funnel (`LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent`) shows exactly which step loses players, which addresses the bounce-rate signal directly ([Funnel events](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/funnel-events)). Economy events track every source and sink of the in-game currency, and the dashboard shows whether the economy stays near balance ([Economy events](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/economy-events)). Custom events, **up to 100 per game**, carry up to three string fields with **8,000 unique combinations at most**. Put the variety in fields, such as one `DreamCaught` event with a rarity field, not one event per creature ([Custom events](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/custom-events); [Custom fields](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/custom-fields)). Roblox has no spatial heatmap. A workable substitute is to log `PlayerRemoving` and theft events with a coarse zone field ("Bedroom-Attic") and read the clusters in Explore. This is untested but uses only documented features. Thumbnail Personalization accepts 2–5 thumbnails and shifts impressions to each segment's winner. It averaged **+8.5% lift in qualified play-through rate** in Roblox testing, and it is free from launch ([Roblox Docs: Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails)). In-game Experiments need roughly 1,000+ DAU before results are useful ([Experiments](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/experiments.md)), so early on, compare funnels before and after each change.

## One warm lamp in a cool room: the visual rule for Steal a Dream

None of the sources measured palettes or lighting in the top games. Their look is described only as bright, saturated and readable on phones, and that description is not sourced. The strongest guidance comes from colour theory and from Roblox's own docs. Treat the palette as a hierarchy: **about 60% quiet dominant, 30% secondary and 10% accent**. The dominant gives the eye "a resting place" and the accent "draws attention" ([Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/colour-theory-and-application/proportion-and-the-60-30-10-rule)). In interiors the dominant goes on walls and floors, the secondary on furniture and textiles, and the accent on small decor ([Setting for Four](https://www.settingforfour.com/?p=134)), which maps directly onto a bedroom map. Here the 10% belongs to dream creatures, collectibles and steal targets, so players learn that saturated, glowing things are interactive. Roblox's UX guidance says the same: vibrant colour for crucial elements, muted for the rest ([Roblox UI/UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)). Pixar's colour scripts add the time dimension. *Up* drains its saturation after Ellie's death and brings it back when the adventure starts ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/503975/cool-stuff-lou-romanos-color-script-for-pixars-up/)). Each bedroom or dream zone can work the same way, with its own key hue on a shared set of ramps.

The **@blender.community** Instagram account (295K followers, about 249 posts) reposts community renders, mostly photoreal and cinematic rather than game assets. A direct look at about 18 grid thumbnails ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/blender.community/)) showed the same few habits in every strong image. Each commits to **2–3 hues plus one accent**. Each places **a single warm light (a window, campfire or lantern) in a cool or dark scene** as the focal point, and uses **fog to separate foreground from background**. Hero objects have clear silhouettes, and **value contrast comes before colour**. The stylized pieces keep one saturation level everywhere, so nothing looks pasted in. The sample is small and no captions or engagement data were read. Still, the pattern matches what Blender Guru teaches about light, shadow and volumetrics ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2012/09/04/tutorial-mastering-lighting/)). For Steal a Dream, that means a cool blue-violet night ambient, one warm PointLight pool per bedroom (lamp or nightlight), Atmosphere haze to fade distant furniture, and dream creatures as the brightest things on screen. Take the lighting and palette discipline from these renders, not their polygon counts. Roblox on a phone needs few lights and low-poly meshes.

Most older tutorials get one thing wrong: **`Lighting.Technology` is deprecated**. It has been superseded by `LightingStyle` (Realistic or Soft) and `PrioritizeLightingQuality`, both script-writable since the September 2026 engine update, so a preset module can set them at runtime ([Lighting class](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Lighting); [Technology enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/Technology.md)). Roblox's official cozy-interior tutorial shows how small the useful numbers are. It uses **ExposureCompensation 0.5 instead of Brightness**, ColorCorrection Contrast 0.05 and Saturation 0.1, SunRays Intensity 0.023, and a candle PointLight at Brightness 0.7 in warm (255,202,156). It also makes walls at least 1 stud thick so light does not leak ([Enhance indoor environments](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/lighting/enhance-indoor-environments)). The two presets below are starting values calibrated to that tutorial. They are a synthesis, not Roblox defaults, and need tuning in Studio.

| Property | Night bedroom / dream | Day lobby ("waking world") |
|---|---|---|
| LightingStyle / PrioritizeLightingQuality | Realistic / true | Realistic / true |
| ClockTime | 0–1 | 13–14.5 |
| Ambient / OutdoorAmbient | (40,35,70) / (70,60,120) | (90,90,100) / (150,160,180) |
| ColorShift_Top | (180,190,255) cool moon | (255,240,215) warm sun |
| ExposureCompensation | 0.2–0.5 | 0–0.2 |
| ShadowSoftness | 0.4–0.6 | 0.2–0.3 |
| Atmosphere Density / Haze / Color | 0.35–0.45 / 1.5–2.5 / (120,100,200) | 0.25–0.3 / 0.5–1 / (200,220,255) |
| ColorCorrection | Contrast 0.08, Saturation 0.1–0.2, Tint (235,230,255) | Contrast 0.05, Saturation 0.1, Tint white |
| Bloom | Intensity 0.6–1, Threshold 0.9–1.2 (only Neon accents bloom) | Intensity 0.3, Threshold 1.5+ |
| Local lights | Warm PointLights (255,200,150) at 0.7–2, shadows on; cool window SurfaceLights (150,170,255) | Few or none |

For the Pixar-like low-poly look, default to **SmoothPlastic or flat-coloured MeshParts** fed by the palette. Use Neon only inside the 10% accent budget. For hero assets (creatures, beds) build **grayscale SurfaceAppearance maps with `AlphaMode = TintMask`**. `SurfaceAppearance.Color` multiplies the texture **at no extra performance cost**, so one map can wear every rarity colour ([SurfaceAppearance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/surface-appearance)). Bevelled edges of two or three segments catch the light and make low-poly read as Pixar rather than blocky (practitioner inference). For icons that pop, Roblox's guidance is to keep one stat in one colour across text, icons and bars, to use one close-button design in one place, and to give buttons visible hover, pressed and released states ([Roblox UI/UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)). Top simulator-style UIs get their look from UICorner, a thick dark UIStroke with ApplyStrokeMode set to Border, and a vertical UIGradient ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/step-by-step-shop-gui/1726856)). One icon style (glossy Blender renders with a thick dark outline on a rarity-coloured badge), at most two fonts, and **rarity colours repeated on the nameplate, icon badge, world aura and shop tile** make up the consistency system.

### Three palettes to choose from

These are design proposals built from the research, not findings. Each puts accents at least about two value steps above the dominant colours so creatures pop at night. Each must pass a grayscale test: temporarily set ColorCorrection Saturation to -1 and check that the creatures are still the brightest things on screen.

| | A — Moonlit Nursery | B — Candy Dream | C — Deep Dream |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feel | Cozy, Pixar warm-in-cool; broadest appeal | Pastel, younger, brighter night | High contrast, slightly spooky; older kids |
| Dominant 60% (walls, floor, sky) | `#1E1B3A` `#2E2A5C` `#463F7A` `#6B63A8` | `#3A2E6E` `#5B4B9A` `#8F7FD1` `#C9BFF2` | `#0F1226` `#1B2140` `#2B3466` `#41508F` |
| Secondary 30% (furniture, fabric, lamp pools) | `#B8774A` `#E58F7B` `#F2C6A0` `#FFC98A` | `#FF9EC4` `#FFD1DC` `#9EE6CF` `#FFF3C7` | `#6E4A7E` `#A45C7A` `#D98E6A` `#F2B880` |
| Accent 10% (creatures, collectibles) | `#7CF5FF` `#FFE066` `#FF6FD8` | `#FFF04D` `#3DFFB5` `#FF4F8B` | `#00E5FF` `#FFD23F` `#FF3D7F` `#9DFF5C` |
| Risk | Lowest | Pastels compress value; most likely to fail the grayscale test | Can read too dark on cheap phone screens |

All three share one rarity ladder: Common `#B8C0CC`, Uncommon `#5BD46A`, Rare `#4DA3FF`, Epic `#B46CFF`, Legendary `#FFB627`, Mythic `#FF4F8B`, and Dream/Secret `#7CF5FF` with a gradient. **Palette A is the recommended default.** It matches the warm-lamp-in-cool-night pattern of the Blender renders and the official cozy-interior lighting, and it has the most value range. Palette C works well as a "Nightmare" zone or event variant inside A, rather than as a whole game. Test palette B as a seasonal skin rather than the base.

## Build order: palette module, two lighting presets, a dozen events

**Step 1 is the Palette module**, a `ModuleScript` at `ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Palette`. It returns a frozen table of `Color3.fromHex` tokens grouped as `world`, `warm`, `accent`, `rarity` and `ui`. Every build script, UI and effect reads from it, and a raw `Color3.fromRGB` anywhere else counts as a review failure. Mirror it in Blender as a 16×16 swatch image plus one Principled material per token (roughness about 0.7, metalness 0), so models UV onto the swatches and a recolour means swapping one image. **Step 2 is a `LightingPresets` module** holding the two tables above. A small zone script tweens Lighting, Atmosphere and ColorCorrection between them as a player walks from the day lobby into a bedroom or falls asleep. That tween acts as the real-time colour script. **Step 3 is analytics**, wired before the first public test so the first cohort is measured.

| Event | Method | Fires when | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onboarding 1–8: Joined → Entered bedroom → First sleep → First creature caught → Creature placed (first income) → First steal attempt → Opened shop → Second sleep | `LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent` | Each first-time milestone, server-validated | Where do new players bounce in the first 3 minutes? |
| `SleepStarted` (field: zone) | `LogCustomEvent` | Player falls asleep | Which bedrooms pull players into the core verb? |
| `DreamCaught` (fields: rarity, zone) | `LogCustomEvent` | Creature acquired | Are rare reveals frequent enough to excite? |
| `StealAttempt` / `StealSuccess` / `StealDefended` (field: zone) | `LogCustomEvent` | Raid outcomes | Is theft balanced, and is it the top engagement moment? |
| `CreatureLost` (field: rarity) | `LogCustomEvent` | Player is robbed | Does losing precede quitting? Pair with exit logging |
| `SessionEnd` (value: minutes; field: last zone) | `LogCustomEvent` on `PlayerRemoving` | Player leaves | DIY heatmap of where sessions end |
| `ReturnedOfflineDreams` (value: hours away) | `LogCustomEvent` | Player collects incubated dreams | Does the offline hook drive D2–7 returns? |
| DreamDust in and out | `LogEconomyEvent` (Source/Sink) | Every currency change | Is the economy inflating, and what do players buy? |
| Shop checkout | `LogFunnelStepEvent` with a GUID session | Open → view item → purchase | Where do purchase attempts drop? |

In parallel, upload 2–5 honest thumbnails showing a glowing creature being carried out of a sleeping kid's bedroom, and let Thumbnail Personalization pick winners. After each weekly update, compare the onboarding funnel and D1 against the previous week.

The creator learns by building, so the path below has him type every script himself. Each week ends with something running in Studio, and each step leans on official Roblox docs cited above.

| Week | Learn | Build himself | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Luau basics: variables, tables, functions, `ModuleScript` and `require` | The Palette module; a script that colours a test room from tokens | Changing one hex code recolours the whole room |
| 2 | The Lighting service, Atmosphere, ColorCorrection, Bloom, `TweenService` | The LightingPresets module and a zone trigger that tweens between night and day | Walking through a door changes the mood smoothly; the grayscale test passes |
| 3 | Blender low-poly: bevels, UV-to-swatch palette texture, FBX export, MeshPart import | One bed, one lamp, one dream creature using the palette texture | The creature is the brightest object in a grayscale screenshot at phone size |
| 4 | Server vs client, `RemoteEvent`, validating client input on the server | The sleep → catch → place loop with server-side checks | The loop works in a 2-player Studio test |
| 5 | `AnalyticsService` `Log*` methods, funnels, custom fields | The onboarding funnel and the event table above | Published private test shows events in Creator Hub (allow 24 h) |
| 6 | UI: UICorner, UIStroke (Border mode), UIGradient, UIScale press feedback | One button recipe and one rarity-badge icon, reused everywhere | Shop and HUD use only palette tokens and one button style |

## Conclusion

The research changes the creator's main question from "how do we make it look as good as the top games" to "how do we make tomorrow's visit inevitable." The top steal games were never visually ambitious. Their weakness is long-term retention, and since mid-2026 Roblox scores games on long-term retention. That opens a gap for a steal game whose core verb is also its return hook: sleeping is offline progress, and the bedroom is both the base and the showpiece. A visual identity built on one warm lamp in a cool room also gets the game past Roblox's "closely resembles" penalty, which eggs and brainrots will not.

Two uncertainties remain. Nobody has published how lore or visual fidelity affects Roblox retention, so the Pixar-style bedrooms are a bet on differentiation, not a proven lever. The 16+ launch gate also means the first 250 engaged players will be older than the intended audience. The cheapest way to resolve both is the analytics wired in week 5. Instrument the first private test properly, then let the first cohort's funnel and D1 data decide where the weekly updates go.
